Privacy Policy

Cartographer's Lie · Last updated 17 August 2026

Cartographer's Lie is a daily deduction puzzle game published by Kevin Marsh ("I", "me"). This policy describes exactly what the app collects, why, where it goes, and how to get rid of it. It is written to match what the app's database actually stores — not a generic template.

1. Who is responsible

Kevin Marsh is the data controller for Cartographer's Lie. Contact: info@mieruconsulting.ai.

2. What the app collects

2.1 Account identifier

You sign in with Sign in with Apple. I receive a stable, app-specific user identifier from Apple, which is stored as your account ID. If you choose to share your email address at sign-in, Apple may supply either your real address or a private relay address; if you choose to hide it, I never receive your real address. I do not receive your Apple ID password, your Apple account name, your payment details, or your device's advertising identifier.

2.2 Profile

A display handle you choose, a randomly generated friend code used to add friends, and your account creation date.

2.3 Gameplay history

2.4 Ratings you give

If you rate a puzzle, your 1–5 star vote is stored against your account and that puzzle. Votes are aggregated into a puzzle's public score; your individual vote is not displayed to other players.

2.5 Social connections

Friendships you form, setters you follow, and duels you take part in — including who challenged whom, who won, and the reason the duel ended.

2.6 Puzzles you create

If you set puzzles, your setter display name, setter rating, tier and reputation are stored, and your setter name is shown publicly alongside puzzles you publish.

2.7 Membership status

Whether you hold an active Guild membership, which product you bought, and when the current period ends. Payments are processed entirely by Apple. I never see or store your card number, billing address, or any other payment instrument.

What the app does not collect: location data, contacts, photos, microphone or camera access, health data, browsing history, advertising identifiers, or biometric data. The app contains no advertising and no third-party trackers.

3. Why I'm allowed to hold it

For users in the UK, EU and other regions with equivalent law, the lawful bases are: performance of a contract for your account, gameplay history, ratings and membership status, since the game cannot deliver a rated, streak-tracking puzzle without them; and legitimate interests for preventing cheating and abuse. Where consent is the basis, you may withdraw it by deleting your account.

5. How long it is kept

Your account and its gameplay history are kept for as long as your account exists. When you delete your account, your profile, submissions, ratings, streaks, social connections and rating history are deleted. Puzzles you published as a setter may remain available to other players, with your setter name removed. Anonymised, aggregate statistics that cannot identify you — such as a puzzle's overall difficulty score — are retained.

6. Your rights and how to use them

You can delete your account and all associated data from within the app; this is immediate and does not require contacting me. You may also request access to your data, correction of it, a portable copy, or restriction of processing, by writing to info@mieruconsulting.ai. I will respond within 30 days.

If you are in the EU or UK you may complain to your local supervisory authority. If you are a California resident, you have the rights described under the CCPA/CPRA, including the right to know and the right to delete; I do not sell or share personal information as those terms are defined there.

7. Children

Cartographer's Lie is not directed at children under 13, and I do not knowingly collect personal information from them. If you believe a child has created an account, write to me and I will delete it.

8. Security

All traffic between the app and the server is encrypted in transit. Database access is restricted by row-level security policies so that your data is readable only by your own authenticated session, except where the game deliberately shows information publicly — leaderboard standings, setter names on published puzzles, and handles visible to friends. No system is perfectly secure, and I cannot guarantee absolute security.

9. Changes

If this policy changes materially, the date at the top will be updated and the change will be noted in the app before it takes effect.
